# Reusable survey templates

Starting points for the six survey categories sold on `/survey`. Each template
is a structure, not a fixed questionnaire: adapt wording and options to the
client's decision, language and sample.

Shared rules for every template:

- Get consent before the first question and record it.
- Ask only what the analysis will use.
- Keep personally identifying data out of the analysis dataset.
- Pilot the instrument on 10–30 responses before full fieldwork.
- Back-check at least 5–10% of responses.
- Do not fabricate, leading-prompt or fill in missing answers.

## 1. Political and public opinion survey

**Respondent:** Adult voter in the constituency.
**Mode:** Face-to-face, 12–18 minutes.
**Sample:** Random or quota sample by ward/booth; report the frame.

1. Are you registered to vote in this constituency?
2. How likely are you to vote in the next election? (certain / likely / unlikely / will not vote)
3. What is the most important issue facing your area? (open, then code)
4. How would you rate the availability of drinking water, roads, drainage and street lighting?
5. Do you feel the current representative is accessible? (yes / somewhat / no / don't know)
6. Which party or candidate would you vote for if the election were held today?
7. How has your opinion of the incumbent changed in the last year? (improved / same / worsened)
8. Have you received information from any party or candidate in the last month?
9. Demographics: age band, gender, education, occupation, ward.
10. Consent to be re-contacted for a follow-up? (optional, recorded separately)

**Checks:** No campaigning language, no candidate logos on the form, no
interviewer prediction of the result, and a clear margin-of-error note in the
report.

## 2. Product and brand survey

**Respondent:** Current, lapsed or prospective customer.
**Mode:** In-person intercept, phone or online, 8–12 minutes.

1. Which brands in this category do you know? (unaided)
2. Which brands have you seen or heard about in the last three months? (aided)
3. Which brand do you use most often?
4. What do you like and dislike about it? (open)
5. Have you used [product]? If yes, how often?
6. How satisfied are you with [attribute]? (1–5 scale)
7. How likely are you to recommend it? (0–10)
8. What would make you switch to another brand?
9. Where do you usually buy or research this category?
10. Demographics and consent.

**Checks:** Rotate brand lists, avoid priming with the client's name first, and
distinguish users from non-users before analysis.

## 3. Customer satisfaction survey

**Respondent:** Recent customer or service user.
**Mode:** Phone, SMS/WhatsApp link or email, 6–10 minutes.

1. When did you last use [service]?
2. How satisfied are you with the overall experience? (1–5)
3. How satisfied are you with staff behaviour, waiting time, price and resolution? (1–5 each)
4. Was your issue resolved on the first contact?
5. How much effort did it take to get help? (1–7, very easy to very difficult)
6. How likely are you to recommend us? (0–10)
7. What is the one thing we should improve first? (open)
8. May we contact you about this response? (optional)

**Checks:** Map every question to an operational owner, report response rate,
and do not present a self-selected online sample as representative.

## 4. Government scheme and social impact survey

**Respondent:** Intended beneficiary or household.
**Mode:** Face-to-face, 15–25 minutes.
**Sample:** Beneficiary list or household frame, with a comparison group where possible.

1. Are you aware of [scheme]?
2. How did you hear about it?
3. Have you applied? If not, why not?
4. Did you receive the benefit? When?
5. How much time and cost did the application take?
6. Did you face any difficulties with documents, officials or the portal?
7. How has the scheme changed your household situation? (open + rating)
8. Is there anyone in the household who still needs the scheme but is not covered?
9. Demographics, location and consent.

**Checks:** Do not promise eligibility; record refusals and missing responses;
protect beneficiary identity in reports; follow the client's data-sharing rules.

## 5. Household survey

**Respondent:** Household head or a knowledgeable adult member.
**Mode:** Face-to-face, 20–30 minutes.
**Sample:** Random household selection within a defined area.

1. Household size, composition and years at the address.
2. Housing type, ownership and access to water, sanitation and electricity.
3. Main income sources and monthly income band.
4. Employment of working-age members.
5. Education of children and adults.
6. Health expenditure and access to a health facility.
7. Distance and travel time to school, health centre, bank and market.
8. Assets owned (fixed list, yes/no).
9. Shocks in the last 12 months and how the household coped.
10. Consent and callback details.

**Checks:** Use a household roster, randomise respondent selection where
required, and keep income answers in bands rather than exact amounts.

## 6. Academic research survey

**Respondent:** Students, faculty, professionals or the population defined in the study.
**Mode:** Online or face-to-face, 10–20 minutes.

1. Screening questions against the study's inclusion criteria.
2. Institution, course or role (as required by the study).
3. Construct items on the study's scale, with the original wording and citations.
4. Behaviour or usage questions relevant to the hypothesis.
5. Open qualitative follow-up (if the design includes it).
6. Consent, anonymity statement and withdrawal information.
7. Optional contact for follow-up (stored separately).

**Checks:** Preserve scale validity and reliability, pre-register the analysis
where possible, report limitations, and never fabricate data, results or
citations.

## Operational template

Every project brief should record:

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Decision the research supports | Whether to expand a scheme to 4 new districts |
| Population and sample frame | Beneficiary list, 1,200 households, 12 clusters |
| Instrument | Template 4, Telugu + Hindi, 20 minutes |
| Field plan | 6 enumerators, 2 supervisors, 10 days, daily back-checks |
| Quality rules | GPS/photo consent, duplicate check, 10% back-check |
| Deliverables | Clean dataset, codebook, report, dashboard, presentation |
| Compliance | Consent, retention period, data-sharing agreement |
